Rules

A two-player immunology card battle

Overview

Two players face off in a series of rounds. Each player is assigned a side by a coin flip — Immune or Disease — and chooses cards from their side. Each round, both players play a card. The card with the better matchup deals damage to the other. Rounds continue until one player wins the majority.

Setup

  • Choose the number of rounds (3 or 5).
  • A coin flip decides which side each player commands. The result is final for the whole match.
  • Player 1 picks up to 5 regular cards of their side. Player 2 does the same, from the remaining cards.
  • Each player then picks one wild card matching their side.
  • Wilds are one-use only, armed before a Battle.

Cards

Each card has a base power (offense) and HP (how much damage it can absorb). Cards also have strong and weak matchups against opposing cards, expressed as multipliers:

MultiplierMeaning
x2.0Strong against — deals double damage
x1.0Neutral — normal damage
x0.5Weak against — half damage

Immune cards

Immune Natural Killer, Antibody, T Cell, Macrophage, Neutrophil. These are the body's defenders. Each has a specific mechanism and strengths against certain diseases.

Disease cards

Disease Flu, Tuberculosis, Malaria, Ringworm, Salmonella. These attack the body. Each has a base power and specific weaknesses to immune cells.

Wild cards

Wild One-use cards that tilt a single round:

  • Bacteriophage — weaken one of the opponent's benched disease cards by half its HP.
  • Antibiotics — damage every benched card on both sides. Disease cards take 4 damage, immune cards take 2.
  • Antibiotic Resistance — halve the damage the opponent's played card deals this round.
  • Superbug — double the damage your played card deals, if it is a disease card.

Match flow

  • Learn phase: each player scans or picks their cards. A lesson is generated for each card.
  • Wild phase: each player picks a wild card.
  • Match: for each round, Player 1 scans their card, then Player 2. Both press Battle.
  • Resolution: damage is traded until one card is destroyed or HP is compared. Ties go to the defender.
  • Win: the first player to win the majority of rounds wins the match.

Scanning

Cards can be scanned using your device's camera. Point the camera at a card, and the system will try to recognise it. If recognition fails, a manual picker lets you choose the card by name.

Tip: hold the card steady, fill a good portion of the frame, and make sure the lighting is even. If recognition keeps failing, fall back to the manual picker.

Asking questions

After learning or playing a card, you can ask the tutor follow-up questions in the chat panel. The tutor answers with 2 to 4 sentences, always grounded in the real biology behind the card.

Winning

Play the best matchups. A card that is strong against the opponent's played card deals double damage; a card that is weak against it deals half. Choosing wisely each round is the whole game.
